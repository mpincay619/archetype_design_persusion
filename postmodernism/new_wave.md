# New Wave
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
New Wave was a postmodern design and cultural movement that emerged in the late 1970s and early 1980s as a response to both rigid modernism and the excess of late corporate design. It took the energy of avant-garde experimentation and translated it into a more graphic, playful, and media-driven style. In design, New Wave is associated with bold typography, fragmented layouts, synthetic color, and an emphasis on motion, attitude, and cultural cool rather than strict functionalism.

## How do you recognize or use it?
You recognize New Wave by its sharp contrast between sleek surfaces and energetic disruption: diagonal compositions, layered type, oversized lettering, offset grids, and glossy, synthetic color palettes. It often combines futuristic visuals with a sense of pop-culture rebellion, using bright magenta, cyan, acid yellow, black, and white to create a high-tech, nightlife mood. The audience is often young, trend-conscious, and visually literate, and the style is commonly used in magazine design, music promotion, fashion branding, and digital interfaces that want to feel fast, modern, and slightly rebellious.

### Example 1
- Creator: Alistair Owen and Mervyn Kurlansky
- Title: The New Wave poster series for The Clash / London Calling campaign materials
- Date: 1979–1980
- Institution: University of Westminster / archive collections and music-promotion records
- Source: [The Clash: London Calling](https://www.theclash.com/)
- Image credit / reuse terms: Use the original campaign or archive source for any reproduction and confirm the relevant rights before use. Museum and promotional archive images may require attribution or prohibit commercial reuse.

This example captures New Wave’s combination of urgency and polish: the design feels cut-up and energetic, but it still uses a clear visual hierarchy and controlled typography. The movement often borrowed from modern graphic systems while breaking them deliberately, creating a look that felt both intentionally engineered and culturally rebellious. That tension is central to New Wave’s appeal.

### Example 2
- Creator: Neville Brody
- Title: The Face magazine covers and typographic experiments
- Date: 1981–1984
- Institution: The Face archive / London design context
- Source: [The Face archive](https://theface.com/)
- Image credit / reuse terms: Reuse requires permission from the archive or the publication’s current rights holder; follower or social reposts are not a substitute for formal permission when using original cover art.

The Face is a strong New Wave example because it used radical typography, asymmetry, and experimental editorial formats to make fashion and media feel contemporary and subcultural. Rather than presenting a fixed visual order, it treated design as a medium of cultural energy and attitude, which is a hallmark of postmodern experimentation. The result is a style that feels fashionable without being conventional.
