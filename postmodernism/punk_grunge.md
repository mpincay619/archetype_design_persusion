# Punk & Grunge
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
Punk and grunge are related postmodern design sensibilities built around rebellion, anti-fashion, and emotional rawness rather than polished luxury. Punk emerged in the mid-1970s in London and New York as a deliberately rough, anti-establishment response to consumer culture, while grunge developed in the early 1990s in Seattle as a more wear-worn, slacker-inflected version of the same attitude. Both styles reject smooth corporate polish in favor of visible texture, DIY energy, and a sense that imperfection is part of the message.

## How do you recognize or use it?
You recognize punk and grunge by distressed textures, jagged type, clipped imagery, loud contrast, and a visual language that feels handmade, torn, or intentionally unfinished. Punk often uses red-black-white palettes, photocopied layouts, stencil lettering, and direct political or anti-authority messaging; grunge leans toward muted earth tones, heavy grain, loosely layered type, and a sense of fatigue or urban dislocation. The audience is usually young, anti-mainstream, and skeptical of polished branding. In practical use, the style works for band posters, independent media, activist campaigns, subculture brands, and any project that wants to feel authentic, rebellious, or lived-in rather than premium and controlled.

## Examples
### Example 1: Punk
- Creator: Jamie Reid
- Title: God Save the Queen
- Date: 1977
- Institution: Victoria and Albert Museum, London
- Source: [V&A: Punk and its influence](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/punk-and-its-influence)
- Image credit / reuse terms: Use the V&A attribution and reuse guidance for the image. If a file is copied from an external source, keep the original creator credit and confirm the license or reproduction terms before reuse.

This poster is a classic punk design because it turns political anger into a graphic system of cut-up lettering, collage, and aggressive repetition. The visual aggression is intentional: it is meant to offend the establishment, to feel fast and improvised, and to reject the elegance of traditional poster design. In a hero or campaign context, it shows how punk uses disruption and anti-brand messaging to make a product or message feel urgent and oppositional.

### Example 2: Grunge
- Creator: Robert Fisher
- Title: Nevermind
- Date: 1991
- Institution: Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP), Seattle; Geffen Records archive
- Source: [Wikipedia: Nevermind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevermind)
- Image credit / reuse terms: Retain the credit line for the original designer and check archive or record-label reproduction terms before publication. The source page is for historical documentation, not blanket permission for reuse.

This album cover is a foundational grunge image because it combines a washed-out photographic treatment with a raw, almost accidental mood. The bent, tired aesthetic reflects grunge’s emphasis on authenticity over polish, while the off-center composition keeps it from feeling overly managed or sterile. It demonstrates how grunge can make a product look like it belongs to a lived-in world rather than a carefully staged brand campaign.
