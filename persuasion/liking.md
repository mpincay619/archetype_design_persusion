# Liking
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
The principle of liking is the tendency to say yes to people or brands we feel comfortable with, connected to, or positively about. People are more likely to trust and buy from sources that feel familiar, friendly, and similar to them. In design, this often means using approachable language, warm visuals, shared values, and a tone that makes the audience feel understood.

## How do you recognize or use it?
You recognize liking in marketing that relies on friendliness, relatability, and social proof rather than pressure. It appears in welcoming product copy, casual photography, customer stories, lifestyle imagery, and messages that reflect the audience’s preferences or identity. It works especially well for consumer brands, communities, and products that need to feel trustworthy and enjoyable rather than formal or intimidating.

## Examples
### Example 1: Friendly brand voice
A wellness brand uses copy like “Made for busy people who want less stress” and pairs it with soft photography and a relaxed layout. Because the tone feels emotionally aligned with the audience, the brand becomes easier to trust and easier to like.

### Example 2: Shared values and lifestyle alignment
A coffee brand highlights local sourcing, everyday routines, and community-based imagery. The audience sees the brand as part of their own habits and values, which makes the product feel familiar and desirable before the purchase decision is even made.

This principle works because people are more responsive to brands that feel pleasant, aligned, and easy to identify with. The design goal is not to be flashy; it is to feel familiar, reassuring, and emotionally compatible.
