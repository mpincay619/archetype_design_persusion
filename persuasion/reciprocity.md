# Reciprocity
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
Reciprocity is the persuasion principle that people feel social pressure to return favors, gifts, or kindness. In behavioral science, it is one of the strongest drivers of compliance because people do not want to feel indebted or unfair. In marketing and design, the idea is simple: when a brand gives value first, the audience is more likely to respond positively, buy, or engage.

## How do you recognize or use it?
You recognize reciprocity in free samples, generous onboarding content, helpful checklists, surprise discounts, and customer service that goes beyond expectations. It works especially well for audiences who value trust, ease, and fairness, such as first-time buyers, communities, and service-based brands. In a page or ad, it usually appears as a small but genuine benefit offered before a request: a free template, a trial, a gift with purchase, or a useful resource that feels worth returning.

## Examples
### Example 1: Free sample with a CTA
A skincare brand offers a free 3-day sample pack with an order, then invites the shopper to “Try a routine that fits your skin.” The free value lowers the barrier to action and activates the feeling that the customer should respond in kind by purchasing the full set.

### Example 2: Helpful content before the ask
A SaaS landing page gives away a free workflow template, checklist, or mini audit, then presents a CTA like “Get the toolkit.” Because the visitor already received useful value, the page feels less like a hard sell and more like a reciprocal exchange.

This principle works because it changes the emotional frame from “I am being marketed to” to “I already received something useful.” That makes the later request feel fair, not pushy.
