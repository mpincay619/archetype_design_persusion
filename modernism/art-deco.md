## What is it?
Art Deco is an influential visual arts and design style that emerged in France just before World War I and flourished internationally during the 1920s and 1930s. Characterized by sleek geometric forms, bold linear symmetry, and lavish materials, it celebrated the speed, power, and optimism of the machine age.

## How do you recognize or use it?
- **Characteristics:** Stepped zig-zag forms, sunburst motifs, chevron patterns, metallic finishes (gold, chrome, bronze), high-contrast palettes, and streamlined geometric shapes.
- **Audience & Context:** Luxury goods, hospitality branding, architectural facades, and premium packaging where elegance, authority, and glamour are desired.

## Examples

### Example 1: Architecture
![Chrysler Building Detail](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Chrysler_Building_spire_sunset.jpg/800px-Chrysler_Building_spire_sunset.jpg)  
*Image credit: Wikimedia Commons / Carol M. Highsmith Archive*

**The Chrysler Building (1930), William Van Alen, New York.** Its stainless steel crown of stepped terraced arches and sunburst triangular windows is one of the clearest architectural monuments of Art Deco's geometric, machine-age optimism. [Library of Congress Archive](https://www.loc.gov/item/2011632168/).

### Example 2: Poster Graphic Design
![L'Atlantique Poster](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Cassandre_L_Atlantique.jpg/800px-Cassandre_L_Atlantique.jpg)  
*Image credit: Wikimedia Commons / A.M. Cassandre*

**L'Atlantique Ocean Liner Poster (1931), A.M. Cassandre.** Cassandre used severe rectangular geometry, extreme scale contrast, and streamlined architectural letterforms to evoke the monumental scale of modern transatlantic travel. [MoMA Collection](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/5387).
