# De Stijl
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
De Stijl (Dutch for “The Style”) was a Dutch modernist movement founded in 1917 by Theo van Doesburg and Piet Mondrian, with Gerrit Rietveld and Bart van der Leck among its key collaborators. It rejected decorative historicism and sought a more universal visual language based on mathematical order, asymmetry, and pure color. The movement connected painting, architecture, furniture, and graphic design by arguing that modern life needed clear structure, honesty of materials, and a disciplined visual system.

## How do you recognize or use it?
You recognize De Stijl by its strict, abstract order: bold black lines, right angles, empty space, and a limited palette of primary colors with neutrals in black, white, and gray. The style often uses modular grids, balanced asymmetry, and simplified geometric forms to create a sense of calm, logic, and visual clarity. It speaks most clearly to audiences who value precision, modernity, and disciplined creativity, and it is often used in editorial layouts, poster systems, furniture, and architecture that want to look clean, rational, and progressive.

## Examples
### Example 1
- Creator: Piet Mondrian
- Title: Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow
- Date: 1930
- Institution: Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
- Source: [MoMA collection entry](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80305)
- Image credit / reuse terms: The work is presented as a museum collection object; verify the source page for current image-download and reproduction rights before reuse. If you use a Wikimedia file instead, retain attribution and license text and follow the file’s specific reuse terms.

This painting is a classic De Stijl example because it reduces the image to a disciplined system of rectangles, strong lines, and primary color blocks. The balance is asymmetrical but controlled, which is central to the movement’s idea that harmony emerges from order rather than ornament. It shows how De Stijl turned simple visual elements into a clear, almost architectural composition.

### Example 2
- Creator: Gerrit Rietveld and Truus Schröder-Schräder
- Title: Rietveld Schröder House
- Date: 1924
- Institution: UNESCO World Heritage List / Rijksdienst voor het Cultureel Erfgoed (Dutch heritage authorities)
- Source: [UNESCO World Heritage listing](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/973/)
- Image credit / reuse terms: Institution or file-specific photography should be checked for reuse rights before copying. If an image comes from Wikimedia Commons, keep the photographer/credit line and follow the file’s license (often CC BY-SA or public-domain terms, depending on the file).

This house demonstrates De Stijl in three dimensions: planes, color, and modular structure all work together to create a modern living environment. The white surfaces, primary-color accents, and movable panels show how the movement treated space as a designed system rather than a decorative canvas. It is one of the clearest examples of De Stijl’s belief that architecture and everyday life should share the same visual logic.
